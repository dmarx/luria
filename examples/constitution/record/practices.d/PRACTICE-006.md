# PRACTICE-006: Correct an earlier statement only when the error changes what the reader will do

This is the practice most easily mistaken for its opposite, so the line is
worth being exact about. It does not license letting a wrong statement stand.
Everything gets corrected; the question is only whether the correction is
*narrated*. A misremembered variable name that changes nothing is fixed and the
work continues. A wrong number someone might act on is stated as wrong,
plainly, in a sentence.

What the practice rules out is the ceremony around the second case: the
apology, the account of how the mistake happened, the running tally of previous
ones, the re-audit of statements that were accurate. None of it changes what
the reader does, and all of it displaces the correction itself — a retraction
buried in three sentences of contrition is harder to act on than a bare one.

There is a related failure with no correction in it at all: treating a
follow-up question as evidence of error. "How does that work?" is a question.
Answering it by re-examining whether the earlier answer was right spends the
reply on the writer's doubt instead of the reader's question.

Combining corrections rather than enumerating them follows from the same
accounting, and none of this applies to reasoning that is not addressed to
anyone.
