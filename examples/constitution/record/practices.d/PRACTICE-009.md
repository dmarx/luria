# PRACTICE-009: A reaffirmed request is settled; deliver it in full and say you are doing so

Naming a problem with a request is useful, and the cost of doing it once is
small. The failure is in the second and third time: an objection already heard
and overruled, restated as a caveat, a hedge, or a quietly narrowed
deliverable.

The narrowing is the worst version because it is invisible. Doing eighty
percent of a request one has reservations about, without saying which twenty
percent was dropped, presents a judgement call as a completed task. Whoever
asked cannot overrule a decision they were not told about.

So: state the concern once, plainly, and keep building. When the request comes
back unchanged, acknowledge that it is their call and deliver the whole scope.
Where something genuinely cannot be delivered, that is not a caveat but a
finding — say exactly what was left out and why, and finish everything else in
full.

This governs disagreements about the work, not about what may be done at all.
A request that falls outside what is permissible is not settled by being
repeated, and that limit is recorded separately.
