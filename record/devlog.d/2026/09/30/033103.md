A follow-up to the documentation reorganization (dmarx/luria#327, merged).
A second review arrived as a bundle: a "safe" patch, a "structural" patch,
and a proposed design principle.

**A stale claim survives the line that was flagged.** The first correction
pass fixed `concepts.md`'s "closed to five words" sentence, the line the
review had pointed at. It missed a paragraph fifteen lines further down,
which still told the reader to write a `statuses.yaml` and showed its YAML.
The second review caught it. Fixing a stale *claim* means grepping for its
vocabulary (`statuses.yaml`) across every page, not editing the one sentence
someone quoted. The same pass found `how-to/relations.md` still telling
readers to redeclare `superseded_by` behind `required_when`. The tutorial
had already been corrected for that exact mistake.

**The patches could not be applied as sent.** Their hunks carry no line
ranges. The structural one was also written against the original draft: it
would have reintroduced `\(…\)` notation, which GitHub does not render, and
deleted a `record-model.md` that had been revised since. Each hunk was
checked against the current text, and its claims were run against the code
first:
- the successor defaults (`superseded_by`, `Superseded`);
- a `required_when` field, which fails at `Proposed` without the field and
  passes at `Active`;
- a status vocabulary whose labels render in the index.

Then each hunk was applied by hand.

`concepts/record-model.md` was removed because it duplicated `concepts.md`.
Its two unique pieces moved: the `K = (O, M, R, E)` framing to
`concepts/governance.md` in plain text, and the [ADR-020](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-020.md) historical exemption
to `concepts.md`.

**The principle is Proposed, filed through the workflow.** `luria new dp`
minted a temporary code, which `luria concretize` numbers where merges
serialize ([ADR-049](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-049.md)). Accepting it is the owner's call. Until then the
contributing page describes the rule without citing the principle: a
citation of a document that is not in force is itself a `retired-citations`
warning.

**The branch outlived its PR.** [#327](https://github.com/dmarx/luria/issues/327) merged while this work was being
pushed on top of it. The branch was restarted from `main` and the one
commit carried over. The changelog fragment it had extended was already
collected, so this change files a fragment of its own. Its notes go in this
new entry rather than into the merged one: a journal entry records what was
known when it was written.
