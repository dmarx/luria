title: 'Relation statements folded into the directive grammar'
created: '2026-09-30T05:06:52'
tags: []
---

**Third notation, and the first that is not new syntax.** The link-title
form (`[X](X.md "extends")`) rendered cleanly, but review pointed out it
was a second annotation grammar beside the one luria already has for an
author talking to the tooling from inside prose: the comment directive. An
acknowledgement and a relation statement are the same kind of claim about
a citation. Both say "this citation means something specific, and here is
why", so the relation became a directive named by its field,
`<!-- extends: LIT-007 -->`. The `[[extends::X]]` shorthand stayed and now
expands to the link plus that directive.

**What came for free, and what went away.**
- Scope (`-block` for a paragraph, `-file` for the document), `— reason`,
  `until` expiry, and the rule that a quoted example is not a statement
  were all already implemented. A test pins each one the mechanism relies on.
- `unexplained-ok:` is gone. A statement's reason is its explanation, so
  acknowledging and stating are one directive.
- The one-citation-one-relation limit is gone too, because a citation can
  carry any number of statements beside it.

**Two changes to the directive layer itself.**
- The name pattern was `[a-z][a-z-]*?`, which cannot spell `extended_by`
  or `superseded_by`. Widening it to admit `_` changed nothing else in the
  suite or the repo's own lint, because a directive is only acted on when a
  caller asks for its name.
- The widening made a collision possible. A reference field called `pin`
  would be read as the pin registration, and one ending `-block` as a scope
  suffix. `luria.yaml` now refuses such a field name.

**The gap left open, deliberately.** A misspelt hand-written statement
(`<!-- extnds: X -->`) is not a directive anyone asks for, so it is silently
nothing. That is exactly what a misspelt acknowledgement already is. The
shorthand is guarded: a typed wikilink naming a field the document cannot
hold is not expanded, and it fails the wikilink lint. An unknown-directive
check would close the gap for the whole vocabulary at once, and belongs in
its own change.

**Fired again on `examples/constitution`** (scratch copy, `explain: true`
on `grounds`). The fixer wrote statements after 4 citations
(`[VALUE-001](../../docs/values.md#value-1)<!-- grounds: VALUE-001 -->`) and
pushed up 1 shorthand statement. The typo'd `[[overrides::VALUE-1]]` stayed
unexpanded with a lint error. A hand-added
`<!-- grounds-file: VALUE-006 — the whole practice is a cost to the reader -->`
cleared PRACTICE-005's unexplained finding by its reason alone (7 → 6).
