---
title: 'explain: gains a weaker strength, and true now means it'
created: '2026-09-30T06:34:28'
tags: []
---

**The strong push-down mostly produced rote annotations.** Run over
anthology-of-the-sota, `explain: true` as first shipped asked every citation
of an explained relation to carry a `ref::` statement. The fixer duly wrote
one beside every citation, and most of them said nothing the sentence around
the citation hadn't already said. The findings that earned their keep were
the ones about relations the prose never mentioned at all (LIT-237 and
LIT-437 in that record). Those prompted new explanatory prose where there
had been none.

**So there are two strengths.** `explain: true` (or `cited`) is the weak
form. A citation anywhere in the body is taken to serve the relation, and the
only finding is `unexplained-relations` for a code the prose never cites.
There is nothing mechanical to fix, and no annotation to write.
`explain: stated` keeps the old strong form for a project that wants the
relation named at the citation. At both strengths a statement's
`— reason` still counts as the explanation.

**`true` changed meaning without a deprecation step.** The feature landed the
same day and nothing depends on the strong reading, so the key's short
spelling went to the form that is actually useful. Any value other than
`true`, `false`, `cited` or `stated` is refused at config load.

**Fired on `examples/constitution`** with `explain` on `grounds`. At `true`:
7 `unexplained-relations` and no `unannotated-relations`. At `stated`: the
same 7, plus 4 `unannotated-relations` for citations whose sentences
already say which value grounds the practice. Those 4 are exactly the rote
annotations the weak form stops asking for.
