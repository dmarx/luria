---
title: 'Reference-entry detection, fired on the anthology'
created: '2026-10-01T02:00:04'
tags: []
---

**The forcing function had been answered by the bibliography.** The anthology
opted ten fields into `explain: cited`. Asked whether that was making its
prose richer, the measurement said no. About half of the 1427 explained
relations were satisfied by a document's own reference line, `Author (Year),
LIT-nnn — ARXIV-…`, which names the paper and says nothing about it. The
check only bit on the 81 relations whose code appeared nowhere at all.

**The detector reads shape, because the heading is one record's habit.** The
first idea was to skip `## Source`. That fails on every record that names the
section differently, and it would skip annotated entries filed under it.
`luria/bibliography.py` judges each paragraph or list item on its own, by
four signals:
- an author lead that ends at a year, or a classic list of initials and
  surnames;
- identifiers;
- a title, dropped only when a venue or an identifier follows it;
- how much prose is left over.

**What separated entries from prose was case, not connectives.** The first
version asked for two function words among six leftover words. On the
anthology it hid "Two groups, three months apart, the same bug and two
different fixes." and "Two groups, two corpora, two instruments, no citation
in either direction." Both are annotations, terse enough to have almost no
connectives. Counting lower-case words fixed both. Names, initials and
acronyms are capitalised, so authors surviving from a second entry on the
line no longer count as prose either. Now eight lower-case words, or five
with two connectives, read as prose.

**Missed shapes found on the real corpus, each now a test:**
- an affiliation after `et al.` (`Shi et al., Meta (2023)`);
- a lower-case corporate author (`The Movie Gen team (2024)`);
- affiliations containing `of` and `at` (`University of Texas at Austin`);
- `and colleagues`.

The classic formats (APA, numbered, markdown link lists) were never seen on
the anthology, and are in the tests on their own merit.

**Fired on the anthology with the ten fields as configured: 590
findings**, all of them "cites X only in a reference entry (line n)":
- `SOTA.source` 267;
- `SOTA.introduced_by` 253;
- `THEORY.source` 70.

Every other explained field was already discussed in prose. Two checks of
the result:
- **The line-based count disagreed on 133.** Sampled, they are explanations
  that wrap onto the next line, or annotated entries. The block-level
  judgement is right about them.
- **38 of the 590 discuss the paper by its first author's name without the
  code** ("…Hoffmann et al."). That is still a finding worth having: the fix
  is to write the code in that sentence, which the anthology's own linking
  rule asks for anyway.
