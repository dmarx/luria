**[#340](https://github.com/dmarx/luria/issues/340).** arXiv began returning a title in TeX (`$O(n^2)$`), and `normalize()` read it as a different title from the recorded `O(n²)`. The `²` was dropped outright, while `^2` kept its digit behind a separator. Accented letters were also dropped outright. `normalize()` now folds spelling before comparing: it unwraps TeX, applies Unicode NFKD and drops combining marks, names Greek letters (`µ` → `μ` → `mu`, meeting `\mu`), and compares with every separator removed.

**The order of the TeX passes matters.**
1. Formatting commands (`\textbf`, `\mathcal`, …) go first and keep their argument.
2. Letter accents (`\v{c}`) go next. They count as accents only when followed by `{`, so `\cite` and `\boldsymbol` aren't eaten.
3. Then symbol accents and escaped punctuation (`\"o`, `\&`).
4. Only then do remaining control words become their names.

`\varepsilon`, `ε` and `ϵ` all reach `epsilon`, since the variant glyph is the same letter.

**What it must not fold** is pinned in tests: `\mu` vs `\nu`, `n^2` vs `n^3`, and the AdamW nickname. The accepted cost is that `data set` equals `dataset`. For "is this the same paper" that is wanted.

**Not checked on the real case.** `LIT-655` lives in anthology-of-the-sota, which this session can't reach. The issue's own acceptance step covers it: after release, its `source-ok:` should be reported as excusing nothing. No `source-ok:` in this repository or its examples flips.
