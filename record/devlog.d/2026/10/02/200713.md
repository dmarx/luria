**[#326](https://github.com/dmarx/luria/issues/326).** The check compares the working tree against HEAD ([ADR-tmp4osmu](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-tmp4osmu.md)). That makes it a pre-commit check: it is silent in CI and after the commit. That is the moment the issue argued for, and the shallow checkouts rule out walking history anyway.

**The acknowledgement is what needed design.** Every other `-ok:` directive is reported stale once it excuses nothing. Here, though, the check goes blind at the commit, so a correct `summary-ok:` would have been called stale on the very next lint. Two fixes went in together:
- The directive names the version it answers (`summary-ok: v7`), so it cannot silently cover later bumps.
- Staleness is judged only while a bump is uncommitted. A test pins the case the first draft got wrong: an amendment committed with its acknowledgement, followed by an unrelated typo fix. That typo fix made the directive look stale.

**Cost:** one `git diff --name-only HEAD` per lint, plus one `git show` per changed document whose version moved. Unchanged documents cost nothing.

**Fired once on a real case:** [ADR-001](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-001.md) bumped to v3 in the working tree with its summary untouched. It was reported at the `summary:` line, then reverted.
