---
title: 'A typed number: on a temporary document became a duplicate key on the trunk'
created: '2026-10-03T20:51:18'
tags: []
---

The anthology's lint failed on its own `main`, in
`record/decisions.d/ADR-066.md`: a duplicate frontmatter key, `number`. The
bot's concretize commit (anthology 743847cd) had written `number: 66` *above*
an existing `number: tmptzokf`, and nothing in the PR that filed
ADR-tmptzokf had complained.

## Why it happened

`luria new` leaves `number:` out of a merge-allocated document, so concretize
normally has nothing to replace. This document had been written by hand, and
its author typed `number: tmptzokf`. `write_number` was meant to replace an
existing line; its docstring says so, and
`test_write_number_replaces_rather_than_duplicates` tests it. But
`_NUMBER_LINE` matched only `\d*`, the shape the writers themselves produce.
A non-numeric value fell through to the insert branch.

The first theory was that concretize had a separate insertion path for
renamed files. It does not. `concretize.run` calls the same
`new.write_number` that `luria repair` uses, so `repair` had the same gap: a
numbered document carrying a malformed `number:` would also have gained a
second key. One pattern fix covers both callers.

## Why the lint had not caught it earlier

`check_numbers` skips temporary documents outright, reasonably, since they
have no number to compare with the filename. So a typed `number:` on a temp
document passed every check on the branch. It became a violation only once
concretize ran on the trunk, which nobody reviews. That is the wrong place for
a defect to surface. The check now reports it on the branch, and `luria
repair` removes the line.

## Fired on a real case

With the fix installed, `luria lint` on the anthology at 987ae08f, the commit
that filed ADR-tmptzokf, reports
`ADR-tmptzokf.md: a temporary document declares number:`. `luria repair` then
removes the line, leaving `status:` first in the frontmatter.
