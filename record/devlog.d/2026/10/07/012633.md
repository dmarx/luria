---
title: 'A symmetric relation across schemes read as held on neither side'
created: '2026-10-07T01:26:33'
tags: []
---

Found on first use of multi-scheme references downstream. dmarx/nucleation
declared `complements` on CLAIM as `scheme: [CLAIM, THEORY]` with converse
`complements`, and on THEORY as `scheme: CLAIM`, converse `complements`: one
symmetric relation spanning two schemes. With one pair held on both sides,
`luria lint` reported it as one-sided from *both* ends. Each report said the
document lacked an edge it plainly held.

**The cause** was in `relations._held()`, which returned what each side of a
pair declares as `{field: near_reading, back: far_reading}`. For a symmetric
relation `field == back`, so the dict had one key, and the far reading
overwrote the near one. Within one scheme that is invisible, because both
readings are the same reading. Across two schemes the near side's edges
vanished, and every edge looked like the far side's alone, in both
directions. The readings are now keyed by side (`NEAR`, `FAR`).

**It predates [#160](https://github.com/dmarx/luria/issues/160).** A symmetric pair across two single-target fields
(`CASE.compared_against: NOTE` and `NOTE.compared_against: CASE`) fails the
same way; one of the three tests pins that case. Multi-scheme references made
the shape natural to declare, which is why it turned up now.

**Fired before trusting.** On the nucleation working tree, luria 0.36.0
reported 2 one-sided `complements` rows for a pair held on both sides; with
the fix it reports none.
