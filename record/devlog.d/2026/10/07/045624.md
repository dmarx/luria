The second step of the logic-core migration moves the invariant check and
the reading every chain walk starts from (`relations.edges`) to clingo.
`relations.lp` derives `held/4`, a relation read from both sides;
`invariants.lp` derives unbound relation pairs, unbound chain edges, and
components with no common value, taking each component's least code as its
name with `#min`. Python keeps the wording and the order.

**The order was the part to get right, not the logic.** The report prints
findings in the order the old walks produced them, and `findings()`
deduplicates by first appearance, relations before chains. Chain pairs and
lines come out sorted, so `sorted()` reproduces them. A relation pair is met
first at whichever of its two directed readings sorts lower among those the
near side actually holds, so that is the sort key. Without it, a cross-scheme
pair whose near document sorts after its far one would move.

**Parity now runs `luria reports` too.** The invariant findings reach a
user through `docs/reports/unbound-lineage.md`, which `luria index` does not
write, so the step-1 harness could not have seen a regression here. 0.36.0
against itself is still byte-identical with the step added.

**Fired on real cases.** The anthology declares a cross-scheme relation
invariant (`SOTA.source`) and three chain invariants, and has two unbound
lines, one of 140 members. All ten records are byte-identical to 0.36.0
across lint, index and reports. Anthology lint went from about 71 s to
about 68 s. The facts are memoised against the config and a stat
fingerprint of the documents, so a fixer that writes between questions is
read again.

`holds/3` comes from `facts.members`, and `invariants.held` now calls it too,
so the rules and the shell compare the same sets by construction.
