---
title: 'Why index and reports got slower, and the fix'
created: '2026-10-07T07:34:03'
tags: []
---

Step 4's timing table had the anthology's `luria index` at x1.20 and `luria
reports` at x1.40 against 0.36.0, from single runs. Three alternating runs
each confirmed it was real. `index` went from 20.0-20.8 s to 24.5-25.8 s and
`reports` from 15.6-16.2 s to 17.2-18.3 s.

`LURIA_TIMINGS` showed two separate causes.

**The facts were built twice.** Under the render pool, `index` built all
70,238 facts twice, taking 16.4 s and then 4.2 s. Serially (`LURIA_JOBS=1`) it
built them once in 3.0 s. Two threads asked at the same moment, both missed
the memo, and each built a copy, slowed by contending with the other. The
memo checked identity but nothing serialised the build. The facts and each
solve are now built under a lock, and a test has eight threads ask at once
and requires one build.

**Every command paid for every fact.** Timed alone, the HEAD baseline took
2.06 s, from one `git show` per file. `value` and `edge` (32k facts) are read
by no rule, and `holds` covered every field when the invariant rules only
ever read the fields an invariant names. Each `.lp` program now names the
families it reads in a `% facts:` line, and `derive` builds only those. For
`index` and `reports` that is 10,325 facts instead of 70,238. The baseline
reads through one `git cat-file --batch`. `luria facts` still prints every
family.

After the fix, over three alternating runs on the anthology:

- `index`: 0.36.0 took 19.7-20.8 s, the candidate 18.8-19.3 s.
- `reports`: 0.36.0 took 15.6-16.2 s, the candidate 13.7-14.9 s.
- `lint`: 0.36.0 took 74.8-83.4 s, the candidate 60.5-67.2 s.

**A caution about the markers.** `LURIA_TIMINGS` reports wall-clock time. A
solve that runs while the render pool holds the GIL can show 8 s for 0.2 s
of work. Per-phase lines say where time went in one run; whether a command
got slower is a question for the command totals over repeated runs.
