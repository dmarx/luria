title: 'Export standing: one reading of a citation'
created: '2026-10-21T19:44:54'
tags: []
---

**The export had been reading a site with half the facts.** Its
`excused` column was `excused_by is not None`, which is true only for a
directive. Once `lint.inactive_ok` could clear a site with no directive in
the text, a sanctioned citation exported `excused = 0`, exactly like a loud
one. An in-force citation did too, so no query could find "what the lint
warns about" without joining `documents` and knowing each scheme's
`active` word.

**The fix puts the reading on `Citation`, not in `export.py`.** A
`Citation` did not know whether its target resolved or was in force:
`_acknowledge` used both facts to choose a pool and then dropped them. It
now carries both, as `resolves` and `in_force`. With them,
`Citation.standing` and `Citation.cleared_by` are properties, and the export
copies them out ([ADR-tmptj3j3](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-tmptj3j3.md)).

**One thing that looks like a bug and is not.** A `mention-ok` can sit
over an in-force site and over a sanctioned one, because it answers any
site nothing else claimed. So an in-force row can carry `excused = 1`, with
`excused_by` naming the mention. The standing stays `in-force`, because a
mention claims nothing about state ([ADR-105](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-105.md)). Over a sanctioned site the
rule wins, both in the standing and in `excused_by`.

**A sanctioned citation in frontmatter is a real case.** In the export
test, the citer's own `summary:` names the decision it replaced, and the
row lands on the summary's line, not in the body. That is the lint's
reading, since `summary:` is prose and a code in it is a citation.
