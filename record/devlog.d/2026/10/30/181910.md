**The gap.** dmarx/nucleation keeps `record/status-review/worklist.py` for
checks a reported pattern could not express. The pattern subset had only
equality, and `WHERE NOT (x {status: ...})` says "not equal" only about a
node of the match, never about one inside a negation. [ADR-129](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-129.md) had not
rejected predicates. It stopped at equality because nothing yet needed more.
[ADR-125](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-125.md) had rejected a *general* predicate language. That is why the
widening is one comparison inside the node, of the node's own field, and
not a clause language ([ADR-tmpofn2g](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-tmpofn2g.md)).

**Fired on a scratch copy of nucleation** (`git archive HEAD` at f01a22572,
read-only). Two reported patterns were added to its `luria.yaml`:

```yaml
ready_for_ruling:
  match: >-
    (c:CLAIM {status: 'Proposed'})
    WHERE NOT (c)-[:rests_on]->(p:CLAIM WHERE p.status <> 'Active')
    WHERE NOT (c)-[:grounds]->(t:THEORY WHERE t.status <> 'Active')
    WHERE NOT (c)-[:grounds]->(k:CASE WHERE k.status <> 'Active')
    WHERE NOT (c)-[:uses]->(u:TERM WHERE u.status <> 'Active')
    WHERE NOT (c)<-[:objects_to]-(o:CLAIM WHERE o.status IN ['Proposed', 'Active'])
    WHERE NOT (c)-[:countered_by]->(x:CASE WHERE x.status IN ['Proposed', 'Active'])
  report: true
unread_ground:
  match: >-
    (c:CLAIM WHERE c.status IN ['Active', 'Proposed'])-[:grounds]->(w:LIT)
    WHERE NOT (w)<-[:paper]-(n:NOTE WHERE n.status IN ['Read', 'Skimmed'])
  report: true
```

- **Before**, on main: the config does not load:
  `patterns.ready_for_ruling: the only condition is WHERE NOT followed by a
  pattern; ...`.
- **After**: both patterns load, and one solve over the record takes about
  14 s.

**Comparison with `python3 -I record/status-review/worklist.py --top 50`
on the same tree.**

- **`unread_ground`** gave 0 rows, and the script's "Live claims that need
  a work no NOTE reads" also gave 0. Zero equals zero proves nothing
  ([DP-015](../../record/principles.d/DP-015.md)). So a second copy set NOTE-589, the only NOTE on LIT-776, to
  `Superseded`. Both then listed exactly CLAIM-007 → LIT-776 and
  CLAIM-123 → LIT-776.
- **`ready_for_ruling`** gave 84 claims. The script's `unblocked()` (before
  it drops claims a blocker note holds) gave 101. Every difference has a
  named cause, and none is a comparison on one node:
  - **20 claims are in the script and not the pattern.** Each has only
    objections the script counts as *written in*: the target's history or a
    "filed with its concessions" note names the objection on or after the
    objection's date. That is date arithmetic over prose, not frontmatter
    equality.
  - **3 claims are in the pattern and not the script**: CLAIM-058,
    CLAIM-286 and CLAIM-362. None has premises of its own. The script then
    holds a claim back through the ARGs that conclude it, unless one is
    sound. That rule is "no sound ARG", where sound is "no unheld premise":
    a negation inside a negation, which the subset still refuses.
  - 101 − 20 + 3 = 84.

  The script's "Ready for a ruling (1)" also drops claims with a blocker
  note under 14 days old. That reads the clock and the body text, which a
  pattern never will.

**Traps.**

- `WHERE` inside a node and the path-level `WHERE NOT` share one keyword
  token. The contextual lexer keeps them apart, but `not` also lexes as a
  `NAME` where a variable is expected, so `_NOT` and `_IN` take priority 2.
- `_NOT` needed a `\b`, or a variable called `notes` would lex as
  `NOT es`.
- `<>` has to be an anonymous terminal. A named one becomes a tree child
  and shifts the shape of the parse.
- `IN` lowers to a clingo body pool (`status(V, ("a";"b"))`). A pool
  expands to one rule per value, so a match may be derived twice. That is
  harmless, because the match predicate is a set.
