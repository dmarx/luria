**The question.** Published records were getting slow to build and heavy to
open, and the plan is to replace Quartz. The requirements had to come from
what CI had already recorded, not from a local benchmark. So the data is
every `pages.yml` run of luria (365 runs), nucleation (584) and
mathematics-of-meaning (157), with per-step durations for samples of 32, 26
and 1 build jobs, and 23 build logs. The numbers are in [ADR-148](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-148.md)'s
context. In one line: luria holds flat at about 90 s; nucleation's site step
went from 61 s (2026-09-25) to 2731 s (2026-11-25), near-linear in its
artifact (r = 0.985, n = 18); Quartz's emit phase is 84% of the build.

**Traps in the CI data, for whoever reads it next.**

- *Run duration is not build time* in nucleation. A gate job and the `pages`
  concurrency group can hold a run queued: one run reads 2814 s and skipped
  its build. 75 "successful" runs of 60 s or less are gate no-ops. Use the
  build job's own `started_at`/`completed_at`.
- *Logs are tailed to 5,000 lines* by the API, and since 2026-10-03
  nucleation's tail is entirely `upload-pages-artifact`'s tar listing (about
  49k lines on 2026-11-25). Quartz's phase lines were readable for only five
  nucleation builds. This is why the timed build keeps its own Quartz log in
  an artifact and writes only phase lines to the job log.
- *Artifacts are kept for a day* and logs older than about three months
  return 410, so the history can't be re-read later. The weekly `site-perf`
  artifact is kept for 90 days.
- *Quartz rounds its own totals* with `pretty-time`: a 40-minute build reads
  "40m", a 58-second phase "58s". `luria.site_perf stamp` adds
  sub-millisecond stamps to every line.
- *luria's September outliers* (site step 506 s and 631 s on 2026-09-03) were
  Quartz 4.5.2's npm install taking 4–5 minutes, not the build. Quartz 5
  installs in 5–6 s.

**Found along the way.** mathematics-of-meaning has never published: all 157
runs fail in `luria site` with `yaml.scanner.ScannerError: mapping values are
not allowed in this context` while parsing a document's frontmatter, under
luria 0.35.0 and 1.10.x alike. That is a bug in the record, the reader, or
both, independent of Quartz, and should be fixed on its own. Separately, at
nucleation's size the failures are deploys, not builds: three
`deploy-pages` runs timed out at 10 minutes on 360–440 MB artifacts.

**The fix, for now: measure continuously.** `actions/site` takes `timings`
(off by default). Verbose Quartz prints a line per emitted file, so
nucleation's log would gain about 49k lines a build. The weekly run turns it
on and uploads `site-perf`: step durations, Quartz phases, the per-emitter
timeline, the slowest markdown files and output bytes by kind. The
per-emitter timeline is what will say whether social cards, the content
index or the HTML itself dominate emit, which the CI history can't.
`site_perf` was fired once on a real Quartz log and output (strata-g's
record, built locally): it read all four phases, and found 361 social cards
(7.5 MB) and 39 demo videos (99.7 MB) in 127.9 MB of output.

**LadybugDB, checked before proposing it** ([ADR-146](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-146.md)). `ladybug` 0.23.0
wrote a two-node, one-edge database to a single 96 KB file;
`@ladybugdb/wasm-core` 0.23.0 (the Node variant, loading the bytes into its
in-memory filesystem, as a browser would) opened it read-only and answered
the same Cypher query. The browser variant itself wasn't run. Its default
build needs no cross-origin isolation, which GitHub Pages can't provide; the
worker is 25.7 MB raw and 4.4 MB gzipped.
